What is the scariest thing?

No, seriously. What is it? Is it death? The dark? The monster under your bed, in the cupboard? Perhaps it's losing a loved one. Falling out with a friend or a group, losing connection. Getting a bad grade, making a poor choice. 

I don't think it's really any of those things. In some ways, sure, but I think that's just a projection, an example, of the broader idea. I think the scariest thing, something that actually inspires true fear, is permanecy. 

Permanecy is an idea, a concept, that a choice you have made or has been made for you, consciously or not, has changed you and your life, forever, irreversibly. Think of death. Death is scary not because it's a terrible thing, but because it's permanent. You permanently lose your life, your experiences and ideas and consciousness, to an impercievable darkness that will never end. 

Having an accident, a truly awful accident, is something that terrifies a lot of people. What if today is the last day I will ever walk to the bus stop again? Will I ever be able to chew food again? Will my friends and family ever get to have their life back? The permanecy, the irreversible nature of a moment in time, any moment in time, scares me. To have your life divided into moments before and after an event is a horrific comparison, the grand heist of joy. Perhaps the worst kind of permanecy is the one you consciously created. Making a choice, a purposeful decision in the moment, that forever loses you a relationship, a sense of belonging or peace, or an idea of who you are and want to be in the world, is conscious permanecy. It's terrible because maybe you would go back and do it all again completely differently, all the wiser of your choice, but you can't. A moment in time that feels out of your control and out of your hands is easy to to blame on the universe, the gods, the institution. 

But, you can't do that. To understand permanecy, I think you have to live with it. You have to accept that a choice you have made has changed you, whether for the better or the worse, and live with that. Learn from it. Think next time, the next choice you make, and really, all choices you make, what am I going to do if this isn't right? 

I think we all need to be a little less permanent. A little more flexible. Easier to change, grateful of the choices made already and willing to accept that decisions aren't perfect. Sometimes a choice might seem permanent, but try to practice thinking, what's truly stopping this from changing? Is it me? Is it you? Maybe its something we all need to do, to try to not be permanent. A comment said, a number on a test, a toe trod, we can all try to forgive and move on, knowing that each one of us is as equally petrified as the next.
